## Goal
A free, public web dashboard for Federal Circuit argument days. The court posts each panel about one hour before argument (daily schedule is posted by 9:00 a.m.). For each case the dashboard shows:
- how often the panel's judges have sat together, as pairs and as the trio;
- for the issue the user picks from a dropdown, how each judge has voted, by side, compared with the court-wide rate, with sample sizes;
- authorship share and time from argument to decision.

Why build it: per-judge outcome data exists in commercial tools, but this combines (a) panel-morning timing, (b) votes coded by side in the issue's own terms, for every judge including those joining unanimous opinions, and (c) a court-wide baseline, free and public.

## Decisions made (don't revisit without the owner)
- **Source: the court's own website, not CourtListener.** The court's PDFs are clean text. CourtListener could add pre-2015 years later.
- **Coverage starts in 2015.** The court's sitemap is ~complete from 2015 (2015: 516 vs 514 in the court's table; 2016: 543 vs 547). 2013 (~10%) and 2014 (~77%) are incomplete.
- **All case types are included**, not only patent cases. "Side" is defined per case type: patent owner vs challenger/accused infringer; veteran, employee, contractor or importer vs government.
- **Every judge's vote counts**, unanimous or not. Dissents are recorded as votes on the other side. There is no separate dissent section.
- **Votes are shown by the side they favored, in the issue's own terms** (e.g. "voted ineligible"), plus disposition (affirmed/reversed/vacated). "Appellant won" is not used on its own, because either side can be the appellant.
- **Court-wide baseline** is shown next to each judge's rate (a judge differing from the norm is the useful signal).
- **Rule 36 judgments are left out of outcome statistics (option a)** and tracked separately, e.g. as a Rule 36 rate. They are used for sit-together counts (their PDFs list the panel). Outcome labels say "in cases with written decisions".
- **The user picks the issue from a dropdown** on the dashboard. Pulling issues automatically from appeal filings is a possible future feature. Upcoming cases come from the argument calendar, not notices of appeal.
- **Panel, author, date, separate opinions, origin and precedential status come from free text rules, not AI.** AI is used only for issues and outcomes.
- **Fixed, fine-grained issue list** (merging later is free, splitting needs a re-run). Store the label, a one-sentence description and a supporting quote for each issue so later changes rarely need a re-run.
- **Claim construction is coded by its effect on the judgment**, e.g. a vacated noninfringement judgment favors the patentee. Use "other/unclear" when the effect isn't clear (including when the court did something neither side asked for); those count in n but not in percentages.
- **Argument dates:** recordings posted on bulk-upload days (more than 100 in a day: 2013-07-30, 2014-11-12, 2015-07-20, 2015-08-06, 2015-10-30) count as argued but have no date. They are excluded from time-to-decision.
- **Revised/corrected opinions replace the original** when an earlier opinion has the same appeal number(s) and panel; the original is flagged "superseded by revised opinion" and left out of stats.
- **Visiting judges are one "visiting judges" group, not rated individually** (too little data). Stark's pre-2022 panels as a Delaware district judge stay separate from his Federal Circuit record. **En banc decisions are a separate group too.** Both are still classified; the grouping is applied only in the stats/dashboard step. A later version could list a judge's en banc votes as notes.
- **Superseded decisions are excluded from stats.**
- **Decisions with no panel in the PDF (65) are left out of judge statistics** (confirmed by the owner 2026-11-15). Known gap: a January 2019 batch of Rule 36 judgments is missing from sit-together counts for that month.
- **2-judge panels (5) count toward pair counts but not trio counts** in sit-together statistics (confirmed by the owner 2026-11-15; the two judges did sit together, but there was no trio).
- **Issue statistics are matched to forum** (confirmed by the owner 2026-11-15). Forum (from the origin field) is stored with each issue, and the dashboard by default shows a judge's record on the chosen issue in the same forum as the case being argued (the calendar lists the origin), e.g. obviousness in PTAB appeals vs district-court appeals vs ex parte appeals.
- **Pro se flag** (confirmed 2026-11-15): a text rule (not AI) reads the counsel listing and flags pro se parties, so the dashboard can filter them out; pro se jurisdictional dismissals would otherwise dominate veterans, employment and Court of Federal Claims records.
- **Vacated/remanded issues favor the side that appealed on the issue actually decided** (confirmed 2026-11-15). Only issues the court decides are coded; if the court vacates for inadequate agency explanation without reaching the merits, only the explanation issue is coded.
- **Small samples** (confirmed 2026-11-15): issues are stored at full detail; the dashboard decides at build time whether a narrow issue is shown by itself, merged into its group, or as a court-wide rate only.

## Methodology: issue list and coding rules (step 2, approved by the owner 2026-11-15)
**Source of truth:** `data/issue_list.csv` (70 codes in 12 groups: patent validity; claim scope and infringement; patent defenses and remedies; PTAB trial procedure; ITC; trademark; veterans; federal employment; government contracts; other Court of Federal Claims; trade; procedure for any case type, incl. X99 "Other") and `data/sides.csv` (Side A / Side B for 12 case types). Each code has a label for each side and a one-sentence "covers" description that goes into the AI instructions. The workbook the owner reviewed is kept as `review/issue_list_reviewed_2026-11-15.xlsx` (approved without edits). Groups are the levels the dashboard can merge into when samples are small. If more than ~5% of issues in the test land in X99, a code is missing.

**Sides.** Private-vs-private: patent owner vs accused infringer / petitioner / requester; trademark applicant/registrant/owner vs opposer/petitioner/PTO/accused infringer. Government cases: the private party (veteran, employee, contractor/protester, claimant, importer) vs the government. Antidumping/countervailing duty cases use **lower vs higher duties**, not parties, because Commerce may have ruled either way. Interferences have no side. *Clarified 2026-11-15:* inter partes reexams (a third-party requester is a party) go with PTAB trials; only ex parte reexams go with ex parte appeals (applicant vs PTO).

**Coding rules (all confirmed by the owner).** For each written decision the AI records the case type and, for each issue the court actually decided: the code, the side favored (or *mixed*, or *other/unclear*), the disposition, the judges dissenting on that issue, a one-sentence description and a supporting quote.
- Only decided issues are coded; issues the court "need not reach" are not.
- Vacated/remanded favors the side that appealed, on the issue decided (e.g. vacated for inadequate explanation = X06 only).
- Dissents are per issue: a partial dissent counts on the other side only for the issues it covers. Concurrences count with the majority.
- Forfeiture is its own issue (X04), coded for the side that benefits, not as a ruling on the underlying issue.
- *Mixed* (some claims each way) is a separate outcome, shown as its own share on the dashboard. *Other/unclear* counts in n, not in percentages.
- Claim construction is coded by its effect on the judgment (above).

**Test sample** (`samples/classification_test_50.csv`, made by `scripts/select_test_sample.py`, seed 20261115). 35 patent cases (13 PTAB trial or inter partes reexam, 5 ex parte appeal/reexam, 14 district court, 3 ITC) and 15 others (2 TTAB, 3 veterans, 3 federal employment, 3 Court of Federal Claims, 1 Board of Contract Appeals, 2 CIT, 1 district-court non-patent). Drawn at random within each group, except 7 patent slots reserved for opinions with a dissent and 1 for an en banc decision, and patent groups balanced to about half precedential (24 of 50 overall). Left out of the draw: no-panel, superseded and 2-judge-panel decisions, and interferences. The groups come from rough text rules used only for this draw (PTO: "inter partes review/post-grant/CBM", IPR/PGR/CBM numbers, "inter partes reexamination" or a 95/ control number = two-party; district court: "patent" 5+ times = patent, never = non-patent, in between left out); the AI records the case type itself. Dissents and precedential opinions are over-represented, so the test's cost per case is scaled to the whole archive by text length, not multiplied out directly.

**Classifier** (`scripts/classify.py`). One request per written opinion through the Message Batches API (50% off). The system prompt (side definitions, issue list with outcome labels, coding rules; ~4,700 tokens, ~8,000 with the response format) is built from the two CSVs and cached; the user message is the case name, appeal number, date, origin code and the full opinion text (never truncated). The answer is forced into a fixed JSON format: case type, the parties on each side, and per issue the code, description, **ruling** (which of the code's two outcome labels applies), **favored** (side_a / side_b / mixed / other_unclear; recorded separately because for procedure codes either side can benefit from, e.g., "no jurisdiction"), disposition, dissenting judges and a supporting quote; plus panel and author as read by the AI, for cross-checking against the text rules. Effort "medium" for both models. Results go to `results/<sample>_<model>.jsonl` with token counts and an estimated cost.

## Methodology: building the archive (step 1, done 2026-11-15)
Script: `scripts/build_archive.py` (rules in `scripts/extract_header.py`). Output: `data/decisions.csv` and the full extracted text in `data/text/text_<year>.jsonl.gz`.

**Selection.** From the sitemap, take postings whose slug marks an opinion or Rule 36 judgment, posted on or after 2015-01-01, excluding the 2025-03-10 backfill, errata postings and motion-panel orders. Result: 8,023 unique decisions (6,009 opinions, 2,014 Rule 36). By decision year: 2015 659, 2016 791, 2017 747, 2018 700, 2019 707, 2020 690, 2021 652, 2022 536, 2023 556, 2024 609, 2025 739, 2026 636 (to Nov 14), plus 1 revised opinion originally decided Dec 2014.

**Locating PDFs.** The PDF address is predicted from the posting slug: `{appeal}.{opinion|rule_36_judgment}.{m-d-yyyy}.{n}.pdf` (older) or `{appeal}.{opinion|OPINION}.{m-d-yyyy}_{id}.pdf` (from ~2020; lowercase in 2020–21, uppercase later; both are tried). If neither exists, the posting page is read for its PDF link. Text is extracted with `pdftotext`.

**Text rules (no AI)** — each field, and how it is read:
- *Panel:* the "Before …, Circuit Judges." line; for Rule 36 judgments, "PER CURIAM (…)". Handles chief/senior/district titles, footnote markers, letter-spaced capitals ("D YK"), mixed case ("Before Prost, …"), a missing closing parenthesis, and uppercase "AND". Judges not on the Federal Circuit list (the 20 who sat 2015 on: Prost, Taranto, Lourie, Reyna, Dyk, Chen, Hughes, Stoll, Moore, Newman, Wallach, O'Malley, Stark, Cunningham, Bryson, Clevenger, Schall, Mayer, Linn, Plager) or titled District Judge are tagged "(visiting)"; 28 visiting judges hold 292 seats. A judge-name frequency check found no misreads. *Fixed 2026-11-15* (found by the AI cross-check of the full run): a panel line that wraps onto a second line starting with "and" ("CUNNINGHAM, Circuit Judge / and KOVNER, District Judge.") lost its last judge; the rule now continues past a line break when the next line starts with "and". Re-running the rules changed only that one decision (In re Incept, Sept. 2026, now a 3-judge panel).
- *Author:* "Opinion for the court filed by … Judge X", else the "X, Circuit Judge." line after the panel; "PER CURIAM" when unsigned (1,895 opinions); Rule 36 = "PER CURIAM (RULE 36)". An author name matching only a visiting judge is mapped to that judge.
- *Separate opinions:* "Concurring/Dissenting/Concurring in part and dissenting in part … filed by … Judge X" in the first 8,000 characters. *Fixed 2026-11-15* (found by the AI cross-check): "Dissenting Opinion filed by" with a capital O was missed (19 dissents added), and "concurring in part, dissenting in part" (with a comma) or "dissenting in part" was labeled a full dissent (33 relabeled "partial dissent"). No other column changed. *Limitation:* only the judge who wrote a separate opinion is listed, not judges who join it ("in which Judges X and Y join"), which matters mostly for en banc cases. The AI's per-issue dissent list is the source for vote statistics, so this column is used only for cross-checks and sampling. (The 50-case test sample was drawn before this fix, so its pool of dissent cases was slightly smaller than it should have been; this doesn't affect the draw's randomness.)
- *Decided date:* "Decided:", else "OPINION ISSUED:", else "ENTERED BY ORDER OF THE COURT <date>", else the court's "Filed: mm/dd/yyyy" stamp, else the posting date (marked "(posting date)"). `decided_iso` is the sortable form.
- *Precedential:* "This disposition is nonprecedential" → Nonprecedential (3,776 opinions), else Precedential (2,233); Rule 36 always Nonprecedential.
- *Origin:* from the caption line "Appeal from …", "Petition for review of …" (MSPB, arbitrators) or "Petition for Writ of Mandamus to …": TTAB 171, PTO (PTAB/BPAI/examiner) 2,178, ITC 87, district court 1,960, Court of Federal Claims 1,065, MSPB 1,097, Veterans Court 946, CIT 279, Boards of Contract Appeals 132, arbitrator 57. Direct petitions against an agency use the respondent: VA 23, DOJ 14, Office of Congressional Workplace Rights 13, GAO 1. With no caption line (8 decisions, e.g. a PDF whose first page has no caption), the first page is scanned, with "District Court" checked last because visiting district judges' footnotes name one. *Fixed 2026-11-15:* the rule first read only "… from …", so 79 decisions (mostly MSPB petitions, which say "of") had been labeled from a scan of the first page, often wrongly as district court.
- *Pro se* (column `pro_se`, added 2026-11-15): "yes" when the counsel listing names a party followed by "pro se" ("JOHN DOE, City, ST, pro se."). Only the header before the "Before …" panel line is read (first 5,000 characters if there is no panel line), so the opinion's own mentions don't count; those are almost always about an earlier stage ("acting pro se, she appealed to the Board") when the party had a lawyer on appeal (54 such decisions, correctly not flagged). An amicus appearing pro se is not a party (14 decisions not flagged for that reason). The word must be "pro se" with a space ("in prose" in a claim is not a match), and "amicus" must be a whole word (Judge Damich's name had matched). Result: 1,852 of 8,023 decisions (MSPB 725 of 1,097; Veterans Court 449 of 946; Court of Federal Claims 439 of 1,065; district court 108; PTO 86; TTAB 19; others 26). Spot check of 12 random flagged patent/trademark cases: all correct. *Limitations:* the flag doesn't say which side is pro se (e.g. a pro se accused infringer is flagged the same as a pro se patent owner); a lawyer representing themselves counts as pro se; a party pro se on appeal but represented below is flagged, and the reverse is not.
- *Appeal numbers:* from the slug, normalized to YY-NNNN, including consolidated numbers.
- *Argued / time to decision:* a decision is matched to "audio-uploaded" recording postings by appeal number; the argument date is the latest recording date on or before the decision date. **Assumption:** a recording's posting date is its argument date (recordings are normally posted the same day); bulk-upload days give "yes (date unknown)". Results: 5,279 with an argument date, 359 argued with unknown date, 2,385 not argued (submitted on the briefs). For opinions with dates (n = 3,442): median 69 days from argument to decision, 90th percentile 190, max 805.

**Flags** (column `flag`; 1–3% of decisions):
- 65 "panel not found": the PDF has no panel (mostly unsigned pro se per curiam opinions, and a January 2019 batch of Rule 36 judgments whose panel is only a signature image). 63 "author" are mostly the same decisions. Excluded from judge stats rather than adding another source.
- 23 "en banc"; 5 "2-judge panel" (genuine: a judge died or recused; a sixth was a text-rule error, fixed 2026-11-15, see Panel above); 4 "superseded by revised opinion"; 5 "appeal number" (2009-style or miscellaneous-docket numbers like 2015-145); 1 "decided before 2015" (a 2015 re-issue of a 2014 opinion, kept).
- 76 appeals have 2+ opinions (Supreme Court remands, en banc rehearings, second appeals). These are real separate rulings and count separately, except revised re-issues (above).

**Re-running.** `python3 scripts/build_archive.py --extract-only` re-applies the rules to the saved text with no downloads (rebuilds `work/text/` from `data/text/` in a fresh session; tested to reproduce the CSV exactly). Without `--extract-only` it downloads anything missing (resumable). `--no-push` skips the GitHub commit. Background jobs are capped at 2 hours; ask the owner before relaunching a job that hit the cap.

**Test samples** (in `samples/`): 40 recent decisions from RSS, and 98 decisions stratified 7 per year 2013–2026 (seed 20261114), used to develop the rules before the full run.

## Methodology: dashboard statistics (built 2026-11-15, not yet reviewed by the owner)
**Files.** `scripts/fetch_calendar.py` → `docs/data/calendar.json` (the court's monthly calendar PDFs; sessions with panel letter, date, time, courtroom; cases with appeal number, origin code, name, argued / on the briefs; wrapped names joined, "Arguing Counsel" lines skipped; checked: 130 of 130 case lines read for Nov–Dec 2026). `scripts/build_site_data.py` → `docs/data/archive.json` (one row per decision from the text rules, one row per AI-coded issue; 1.8 MB, ~0.3 MB compressed) and `docs/data/quotes/<code>.json` (description and quote, loaded only when a case list is opened). `--test` builds from the 50-case test instead and the page then shows a warning banner. The page is `docs/index.html`, `docs/style.css`, `docs/app.js`: plain HTML and JavaScript, no build step, no libraries.
**Implementation choice (Claude's):** the planned "stats precomputed into static files" became "tables precomputed, counting done in the browser". Reason: the filters (case type, pro se, years) and the clickable case list behind every number then work without rebuilding anything, and every number can be traced to its cases. The counting rules live in `docs/app.js` and are written out here.
**Two modes (added 2026-11-16 at the owner's request):** "A case on the argument calendar" (as below) and "Any panel (no case)", for trying judge combinations, case types and issues with no scheduled argument. In "any panel" mode the session section is hidden, nothing is tied to a case (Rule 36 rate = all cases; the user picks the case type), the notice says "Any panel – judges chosen by you", and shared links carry `m=free` and no session or case. Section numbers were dropped from the headings because the session section can be hidden.
**Methodology page (added 2026-11-16 at the owner's request):** `docs/methodology.html`, a separate page on the same site (linked from the header, a short summary at the bottom of the dashboard, and the footer). Plain-language version of the rules in this file, with live counts, the side definitions and the full issue list read from `docs/data/meta.json` (a ~20 KB file written by `build_site_data.py`, so the page doesn't load the 1.8 MB archive). **When a counting rule or limitation changes, update both this file and `methodology.html`.**
**What the page shows.** (1) Session: date and panel letter from the calendar (defaults to today, or the next argument day), with its cases; clicking a case selects it. (2) Panel: three judge pickers (court judges who sat in the past year first, then others with their last year, then "Visiting judge"); filled in by hand until the morning panel reader exists. *Added 2026-11-15 at the owner's request:* a highlighted notice above the pickers says "Judges entered by you – not from the court", plus, for a future session, that the court has not posted the panel yet and roughly when it will (one hour before the session time); for today's session, when to expect it; for a past session, that the daily schedule shows only the current day. Section 4's case line repeats "Judges: entered by you, not from the court", so a screenshot of the votes alone still carries the warning. When the reader exists, `PANEL_FROM_COURT` in `docs/app.js` switches the notice to "read from the court's daily schedule at <time>". (3) Judges table. (4) Votes on an issue. The whole view is kept in the page address, so it can be shared or bookmarked.
**Rules.**
- *Decisions used for judge statistics:* not en banc, not superseded, decided in or after the chosen start year (default 2015). Decisions with no panel in the PDF have no judges, so they drop out of judge rows by themselves but stay in the whole-court rows.
- *Pro se filter* (default on): leaves out every decision flagged pro se from the judges table and the issue votes (not from sitting together).
- *Sitting together:* number of decisions (written opinions and Rule 36) whose panel includes both judges (pairs) or all three (trio; 3-judge panels only), plus how many were Rule 36 and the latest date. Consolidated appeals decided together are one decision. Visiting judges are one group, so "Prost & Visiting judges" counts decisions with Prost and any visiting judge.
- *Wrote the opinion:* of the written panel opinions a judge sat on, the share they signed. Court figure = signed opinions ÷ panel seats (about 1/3 less the per curiam share).
- *Days to opinion:* median days from argument to decision for signed opinions the judge wrote, precedential and nonprecedential separately (only cases with a known argument date); court figure = all signed opinions.
- *Rule 36 rate:* Rule 36 judgments ÷ all decisions in cases the judge sat on, restricted to the selected case's origin (PTO includes TTAB, since the calendar lists TTAB appeals as PTO) and to argued or on-the-briefs to match the case. No case selected = all cases.
- *Case type of a calendar case* (needed to match forum): a guess, shown as editable. PTO cases: "In re …" → ex parte appeal, otherwise PTAB trial (the user switches to Trademark for TTAB cases). Other origins: the case type the AI assigned most often to decisions from that origin.
- *Issue dropdown:* codes of the case type's group plus the procedure (X) codes, each with its number of rulings in that case type; the default is the most common code.
- *Votes:* each panel judge gets one vote per coded issue: the side the ruling favored, or the other side if the AI lists the judge as dissenting on that issue (a dissent on a mixed or unclear ruling counts as unclear). Shown as the share for side A (in the issue's own words, e.g. "nonobvious (patent owner)"), mixed, and side B; "unclear" votes are shown as (+n) and left out of the percentages. Only the selected case type by default ("Only this case type"; unticked = all case types). Below it, the same for the whole issue group (by side), the fallback for narrow issues.
- *Whole court:* the court's rulings (one per coded issue, not per judge), under the same filters. A tick on each judge's bar marks the court's share.
- *Compared with the court:* a 95% Wilson interval around the judge's side-A share; "voted X more often / less often than the court" only when the interval excludes the court's share; otherwise "within the court's range". Judges with fewer than 10 decided votes are shown grey and not compared. *Limitations:* the judge's own votes are part of the court figure (makes differences slightly harder to find); with ~20 judges × 70 issues some "differences" will be chance, which the page says.
- *Dispositions:* the court's disposition in the cases behind each row (affirmed / reversed / vacated or remanded / affirmed in part / dismissed / other).
- *Case list:* clicking a row lists the rulings behind it, newest first, with the PDF link, the judge's vote (or "Dissented"), the AI's description and supporting quote.
- *Colors:* blue = side A, orange = side B, grey = mixed (checked for color-blind readers); numbers are always shown as text too. Light and dark mode.

## Methodology: automatic updates (built 2026-11-16)
Two GitHub Actions jobs in `.github/workflows/`; both commit to `main` as zljacobs24, and GitHub Pages republishes the site after each commit. A failed run makes GitHub email the owner (its default for scheduled jobs). Both can be started by hand from the repository's Actions tab ("Run workflow").
- **Daily calendar** (`daily-calendar.yml`, 10:41 UTC): `scripts/fetch_calendar.py` → `docs/data/calendar.json`. It commits every day because the file records when it was read; that also keeps the repository "active", since GitHub switches off scheduled jobs in a repository with no activity for 60 days.
- **Weekly update** (`weekly-update.yml`, Saturdays 07:23 UTC): `scripts/update.py`, which (1) re-reads the court's sitemap into `data/sitemap/all_post_urls.txt` (refused if it shrank by more than 5%, a sign of a partial download); (2) runs `build_archive.py --no-push`, which restores the text cache from `data/text/` and downloads only new PDFs, so new decisions get exactly the archive's text rules (including argument dates and superseded flags); (3) sends every written opinion without an AI coding (not superseded, not a download error) to the same classifier, model and instructions as v1, as one batch, waits up to 150 minutes, and saves `results/update_<date>_claude-haiku-5-5.jsonl` (ids `<date>-0001`…); a batch not finished in time is collected by the next run; failed requests are retried next run; (4) rebuilds `docs/data/`. The job is marked failed (owner emailed) if the key is missing, the batch was late, or requests failed, but whatever worked is still committed.
- **Method choice (Claude's): the sitemap, not the RSS feeds** as planned earlier. Same code path as the archive, complete (the feed only holds ~3 weeks, so a missed week would leave a gap), and it lists the argument recordings needed for argument dates. Cost: ~31 small requests a week, 1 s apart.
- **Safety stop:** if more than 300 opinions need coding (a normal week has ~15–30), nothing is sent and the job fails, because that means a bug, not news. `--max-new` overrides it after a check.
- **Cost:** a few cents a week (~$0.0008 per opinion at batch rates). The key is the GitHub Actions secret `FEDCIR_API_KEY` (repository Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions), separate from the cloud-environment variable used in Claude sessions.
- `build_site_data.py` now reads the full run plus all update files; if a decision appears twice, the last successful coding is used.
- The text archive files (`data/text/*.jsonl.gz`) are now written with a fixed gzip timestamp, so a year whose decisions didn't change is byte-identical and isn't re-committed every week.
- Local test 2026-11-16 (`update.py --dry-run`): sitemap 29,410 URLs (1 new), the new opinion (Traxcell v. Cellco Partnership, 25-1496) found, nothing else changed; about 3½ minutes. First GitHub run of the calendar job (by hand, 2026-11-16): reached the court from GitHub's servers, pushed, and the push republished the site (Pages rebuilds on pushes made by the jobs, so no extra deploy step is needed).
